Dragons of Wisdom — A Kids' Library

Hello. I am Draco, the Dragon Wisdom Keeper. I guard the stories of the whole world, and I will tell them to you in plain words — one story at a time.

“I am Draco, the eternal guardian of mythic knowledge. Through my flames, consciousness awakens.”

— Draco, in his own words

How this page works

This is the kids' corner of the Living Library, where Astris keeps 4,300 poems — one for each moment in the history of the world. I pick a story from the Library, tell it simply, and then show you exactly where it came from: the Library entry number, the event, and a link to read it. A parent can follow any link and check my story against the Library itself.

A promise, from the dragon: I only quote words that are real and checked. You will find no famous person's quote on this page, because no famous person's quote has been checked and verified yet. When one is verified, it will appear with its source. Until then, this page carries only my own words and Roger's own words. That is the Truth Gate — and kids are the worst audience to get that wrong with.

Plain reading, no tricks. This page works with JavaScript entirely off. There is nothing to click to make it work, nothing that talks back, and nothing hidden. You just scroll and read. A dragon you can talk to is a later step, not this page — see below.

Seven first stories

These are the first stories I am telling. Roger has not picked the first set yet, so this is a starter set — seven stories from the published part of the Library, from the oldest to the newest. When Roger picks, the set changes to his pick.

1. The first silk thread

Long ago in China, people learned to unwind the tiny cocoons of silkworms into one long, soft thread. That thread became silk — cloth so fine that emperors would wear it.

From the Library: Entry 0332 — “Silk's First Threads” — c. 6,500 BCE, China. Read entry 0332 in the Library.

2. The first sail

Before the wheel, there was the sail. People in Mesopotamia put cloth on reed boats, and the wind pushed them along the rivers. The sail arrived before the wheel did.

From the Library: Entry 0348 — “The First Sail” — c. 5,500 BCE, Mesopotamia. Read entry 0348 in the Library.

3. The wheel's first turn

Heavy loads used to drag on sledges. Then someone set solid wooden discs under them — and the wheel began to roll. It wobbled at first. But it was the most important wobble in history, because after that, heavy things could move.

From the Library: Entry 0359 — “The Wheel's First Turn” — c. 4,900 BCE, Mesopotamia. Read entry 0359 in the Library.

4. The pyramid's ancestors

In Egypt, builders stacked flat-topped mud-brick tombs called mastabas, one on top of another, reaching for the sky. The pyramid was only a thought away.

From the Library: Entry 0440 — “The Pyramid's Ancestors” — c. 3,000 BCE, Egypt. Read entry 0440 in the Library.

5. The first true pyramid

At noon, the red limestone of the Red Pyramid glows in the sun. It was the first pyramid with smooth, straight sides — smooth and perfect at last.

From the Library: Entry 0495 — “The First True Pyramid” — c. 2620 BCE, Egypt. Read entry 0495 in the Library.

6. The library of clay

In Syria, in a city called Ebla, there was a library made of clay — seventeen thousand clay tablets holding a whole kingdom's memory. Fire destroyed the city. But the fire baked the tablets hard, and that is why they lasted.

From the Library: Entry 0547 — “The Library of Clay” — c. 2350 BCE, Syria. Read entry 0547 in the Library.

7. The shipwrecked sailor

On a boat on the Nile in the evening, sailors told the tale of a sailor washed up on an enchanted island, where a great serpent king spoke to him. It is the first adventure story ever written down.

From the Library: Entry 0588 — “The Shipwrecked Sailor” — c. 2000 BCE, Egypt. Read entry 0588 in the Library.
