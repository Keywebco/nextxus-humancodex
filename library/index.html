    About this library

    Each poem sits at one moment in the history of the world, with its date and place. Each poem is an original work. It may be improved over time, but it stays at its own number and in its own place, so you may come back and find it changed. Keep a copy of the version you first read. Over time it will grow beyond poetry into a record of historical fact, with each poem gaining an article that cites its sources.

    Honest limit: these poems are creative work, not a history textbook. For sources, use the Research center.

    Poem 3311, The Unsinkable Sinks (1912 CE, North Atlantic)

    Observation by: A sponsor (sample)

    The poem places the speaker on the Carpathia's deck at dawn, and that detail is worth a closer look. The Carpathia was the ship that reached the lifeboats and picked up the survivors. The poem is a creative work, so the speaker is imagined, but the ship and the rescue were real. A reader who wants the facts of that night can follow them through the sources in the Research center.
